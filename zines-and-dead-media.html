Zines & dead media

RIP media that doesn't survive. Print is forever.

A zine is a small, self-published thing made by hand — stapled, photocopied, traded at shows, sold for a few bucks. There's no prestige, no algorithm, no audience math. A few hundred people get one, and they get it because someone told a friend. It's the last media form that still moves by hand to hand.

I love dead media for the honesty of decay. A dead format is one that only survives if someone deliberately keeps it — the right drive, the right software, a community that cares enough to archive. Obsolescence isn't a tragedy here, it's a test. What still moves when nobody's paying attention is the stuff that mattered.

There's an Ethereum lesson hiding in here. A zine you can't open is just a dead link, and a Jpeg on an IPFS pin that rots when the last node goes offline was never really owned — it was borrowed. The formats I love most — zines, floppies, ANSI art — are all physical enough to fail honestly. Onchain is the fix: render it from the chain and it can't be lost. Both impulses, the handmade and the permanent, are things I want.

WHY BOTH
  • Zine ..... made by hand, dies by neglect
  • Floppy .. physical, rots in a drawer
  • Jpeg .... a URL, rots when the node does
  • Onchain . a function, can't be lost
  • I want the handmade and the permanent.
